What an agentic coder actually does for you

Not autocomplete. A junior RA
who works in a sandbox.

01

Reads the whole project

Point it at your repo or a folder of data + scripts; it builds context across files, not one snippet.

02

Writes, runs, and tests code

Drafts the cleaning script or the regression, executes it in an isolated sandbox, reads the error, and iterates.

03

Proposes a reviewable change

Returns a diff / pull request you approve — the human stays the reviewer of record.

04

Follows house rules

An AGENTS.md file states your conventions (data policy, style) so the agent respects them.

The shift for researchers

The scarce skill is no longer syntax.

It's specifying the task precisely and verifying the result — the same discipline a good referee brings to someone else's paper. The agent is fast and tireless; it is also confidently wrong in ways a novice can't see. Everything that follows is how you supply the judgment it lacks.

The frontier in finance research

LLMs don't just write the analysis —
they've become the variable under study.

Return predictability

Reading the news like the market does

Lopez-Lira & Tang (2023) feed ChatGPT news headlines and score sentiment. The scores predict next-day returns — strongest for small stocks and bad news — with forecasting ability rising in model size.

"Can ChatGPT Forecast Stock Price Movements?" · arXiv 2304.07619
Text as a measured variable

Earnings calls → a capex forecast

Jha, Qian, Weber & Yang (2024) build a firm-level "ChatGPT investment score" from earnings calls that predicts capital expenditure up to nine quarters ahead — text turned into a genuine research variable.

"ChatGPT and Corporate Policies" · NBER WP 32161

The catch: an LLM's reading is a measurement with error. Before it enters a regression it needs real econometric discipline — no training leakage, a validation sample, and a check that the estimate doesn't hinge on which prompt you used (Ludwig, Mullainathan & Rambachan, 2024). One prominent finance-LLM paper was withdrawn in 2025 when its results wouldn't replicate.

Why the whole session hangs on one habit

The same model that accelerates your work will fabricate a citation and never blink.
Measured fabrication
55% → 18%

of citations fabricated by GPT-3.5, then GPT-4, in a peer-reviewed audit of 636 references — invented papers, or real papers with corrupted authors, titles, and DOIs.

Walters & Wilder, Scientific Reports (2023)
It survives peer review

A 2024 finance paper claiming GPT-4 beat analysts at predicting earnings was withdrawn when a coauthor couldn't replicate its own data and analyses. A headline result is not a verified one.

Kim, Muhn & Nikolaev (2024) — withdrawn
The cautionary tale

Mata v. Avianca (2023): lawyers filed a brief citing six cases ChatGPT invented. Judge Castel imposed a $5,000 sanction. In research, that "brief" is your paper.

S.D.N.Y., June 2023

Fluency is not accuracy — the model is optimized to sound right, not to be right. It can even mislead you about its own help: in a 2025 randomized trial, AI tools made experienced developers 19% slower while they felt faster (METR). So we adopt one rule — and build the muscle to enforce it.

Four moves that make AI output checkable

Prompt for verification,
not just for answers.

Move 01

Ground it

Paste the actual text and say "using only this, …". A model summarizing text you gave it hallucinates far less than one recalling from memory.

Move 02

Demand a quote + a locator

"Quote the sentence that states the finding, and give the page or table." A claim you can't locate is a claim you can't cite.

Move 03

Ask for uncertainty

"What did you infer vs. read directly? What are you unsure about?" Force the model to separate its guesses from the source.

Move 04

Re-prompt adversarially

"What would a skeptical referee say this gets wrong?" Counter its instinct to agree with your framing.

prompt→ draft→ verify vs. source→ keep / fix / kill

Every claim the model hands you enters this loop. Nothing reaches your paper until it exits at keep — with a real citation and a locator you checked yourself.

Take this with you

Use AI to move fast. Use verification to stay right. Show both halves of the work.
  • →Accelerate, then verify. The agent drafts; you adjudicate every claim against the source.
  • →A citation you can't locate is a citation you can't use. Quote + page/table, every time.
  • →Keep a verification log. The catches you document are the proof you did the work.
  • →The model is a coworker, not an oracle. Fluent ≠ accurate.
